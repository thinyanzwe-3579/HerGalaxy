001 — The Heart of It All

The Sun

A 4.6 billion year old sphere of glowing plasma, holding everything we know in a gentle gravitational embrace.

The Sun accounts for 99.86% of all the mass in our solar system. Its core burns at 15 million degrees Celsius, fusing 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every single second. The light that warms your face left its surface roughly eight minutes ago, travelling 150 million kilometres to reach you.

003 — Our Home Among The Stars

The Milky Way

A barred spiral galaxy containing somewhere between 100 and 400 billion stars — and our Sun is just one of them.

Spanning over 100,000 light-years across, the Milky Way is so vast that light itself takes a hundred millennia to cross it. Our entire solar system orbits the galactic centre once every 225 million years — a single trip we call a cosmic year. The last time we were in this exact spot, dinosaurs were yet to roam the Earth.

And in all of this immensity — billions of stars, countless worlds — there is still only one of everything that matters most.
